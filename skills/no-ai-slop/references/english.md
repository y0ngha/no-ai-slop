# English editing guidance

Apply alongside the shared principles in SKILL.md. Judge expressions by what they do in this passage. The examples illustrate local edits, not substitutions to apply everywhere.

## Diction and precision

Inspect inflated words such as *leverage, utilize, facilitate, empower, streamline, robust, transformative, cutting-edge, multifaceted, paramount,* and *game changer*. Prefer a familiar word or a supported description of the action. Keep literal and technical uses: financial leverage, robust regression, and a woven tapestry have specific meanings.

- “We utilized the existing checklist to facilitate the review.” → “We used the existing checklist to review the draft.”
- “This transformative update empowers users to export invoices.” → “Users can now export invoices.”
- Keep “We used robust regression to reduce the influence of outliers.”

“Delve,” “foster,” or “harness” is not evidence of AI authorship. Repeated stock wording and vague claims are the issue. Never replace inflated praise with invented metrics. If the source says only “The update improves performance,” do not turn it into “The update cuts latency by 40%.” A review can flag the missing evidence.

Use the same term for the same thing. Do not cycle through “agent,” “assistant,” and “tool” for variety if all three refer to one agent. Preserve distinctions when they name different components.

A familiar professional label can still be too broad. If a usability report measured choosing a save button, “how often people choose the intended action” is clearer than “conversion” for a reader who might think of purchases or sign-ups. Use the action the source actually measured; do not introduce a new metric or claim a measured difference generalizes to customers.

## Sentence construction

Prefer direct verbs when nominalizations add no distinction: “conduct an evaluation of” → “evaluate”; “make a decision” → “decide.” Keep established noun phrases such as “access control” and “statistical significance.”

Use active voice to clarify an action, without inventing an actor. “The team approved the release” is useful when the team is known and relevant. “The files were deleted before the investigation” may correctly leave the actor unknown. “The cache stores recent results” is ordinary English, not personification to remove.

Keep meaningful hedges and small words. “May,” “only,” “just,” “actually,” and “I think” can express probability, scope, correction, or personal voice. “You can simply restore the database” may conceal difficulty; “Only admins can restore the database” states a permission boundary.

Avoid turning every sentence into a clipped declaration. Keep contractions in conversational prose, and do not insert them mechanically into formal writing. Vary sentence length only to improve comprehension or preserve cadence.

## Recurring rhetorical patterns

| Inspect | Edit when it adds no information | Preserve when it does a job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Binary contrast: “It's not X. It's Y.” | State the actual claim directly. | “This counts sessions, not requests” corrects a material misunderstanding. |
| Throat-clearing: “Here's the thing,” “It's worth noting” | Remove the preface. | A personal aside that conveys hesitation or character. |
| Faux insight: “What nobody tells you,” “The uncomfortable truth” | Remove the unsupported claim to special knowledge. | A sourced account of what was overlooked and why. |
| Dramatic reveal: “The secret: better defaults.” | “Better defaults reduce setup work,” if the source supports that effect. | Colons introducing a list, explanation, quotation, or label. |
| Superficial analysis: “highlighting,” “underscoring,” “showcasing” | Cut a trailing clause that only praises or repeats. | A clause explaining a supported consequence or observation. |
| Importance puffery: “a pivotal moment,” “a testament to” | State the event or finding. | An earned ranking or judgment backed by the draft. |
| Weasel attribution: “Experts agree,” “Studies show” | Flag a missing source or remove dispensable framing. | Specific attribution supplied in the source. Never imply an unsupported claim is verified. |
| Fake-strong verbs: “serves as,” “boasts,” “stands as” | Use “is,” “has,” or the actual action. | A verb describing a precise relationship. |
| Negative listing: “Not a tool. Not a platform. A movement.” | Say what it is or does. | Exclusions needed to define scope. |
| Rhetorical questions and fragments | Remove a repeated suspense-and-answer routine. | A real FAQ, teaching question, or deliberate spoken rhythm. |
| Interpretive commentary: “This distinction matters” | Cut it if the surrounding text already shows why. | Explain a consequence when the reader needs it. |
| Stock endings: “The future is already here,” “In conclusion” | End on the supported takeaway or next action. | A conclusion that synthesizes a long argument. |

Do not replace a deleted slogan with a more elegant slogan. Do not make every paragraph follow the same claim–example–takeaway sequence.

## References, metaphor, and punctuation

A metaphor can clarify a mechanism or carry the writer's voice. Replace it when the reader must guess what happens. Keep established terms such as “memory leak,” “bottleneck,” and “fallback”; explaining them may help a novice more than replacing them.

Keep “this” and “it” when the referent is obvious. Name the subject when several candidates compete, especially in excerpts or independently read instructions. Do not repeat a long noun phrase solely to avoid a pronoun.

Em dashes, parentheses, semicolons, and colons are tools. Revise clusters that interrupt comprehension or manufacture drama; a single useful dash is not a failure. Respect the requested house style.

## Paragraph-level example

Source:

> The importer checks column names before upload. It flags missing columns. Furthermore, it is worth noting that the importer is a game changer for validation. Users can fix column names before uploading the file.

Possible edit:

> The importer checks column names and flags missing columns before upload, so users can fix the file before uploading it.

The edit joins an existing action and consequence and removes generic praise. It does not claim that uploads are faster, that all errors are caught, or that a particular team built the importer.
