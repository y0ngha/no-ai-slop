# Label study: findings and proposed next step

We recommend using "Save for later" in the next Cedar prototype for the action that stores an unfinished request. Keep "Submit request" for the separate action that sends it to an approver. The study gives us a reason to test this wording further, but not to release it to every customer yet.

## What we tested

Version A called the saving action "Save draft"; Version B called it "Save for later". Button placement, explanatory text, and the rest of the screen were identical. The prototype did not send real requests.

We wanted to know whether participants could save their work and whether they understood that saving would not notify an approver. A correct click alone does not answer both questions.

Twelve people took part in moderated sessions on 14 and 15 September 2026. All arranged shifts at least once a month, and none had used Cedar before. Six saw A first and six saw B first. Each person completed one unfinished-request task in each version, with different names and dates. Balancing the order did not rule out a learning effect: the first task may have taught people how to do the second.

## What we observed

On first exposure, five of the six people who saw B chose the intended action without a moderator prompt, compared with three of the six who saw A. These groups are too small to give a dependable estimate of a future conversion difference.

Across both exposures, nine participants correctly explained that saving in B would not notify an approver; seven did so in A. We assessed understanding from their explanations. One person clicked the intended action in both versions but still thought a manager would be alerted.

The comments help explain why the labels need further testing. Four participants associated "draft" with email and understood it immediately. Three others called it a technical term or said they did not usually see it in scheduling tools. Several people found "Save for later" more familiar, but two asked whether the system would submit the request automatically later. B worked better on the immediate task while leaving that expectation unresolved.

## What to test next

Use "Save for later" in the next prototype, with "Only you can see this until you submit it." underneath the saved-request confirmation. Keep "Submit request" as the separate sending action; do not add automatic submission.

A second round with eight new participants should check the automatic-submission misunderstanding explicitly before the release review on 6 October 2026. This is a decision about the next prototype, not approval to release the wording to every customer.

The current findings also have limits beyond sample size. Sessions were in English on desktop screens. We did not test translated labels, mobile layouts, or people using screen readers, and the prototype did not reproduce the pressure of a real shift deadline. The results support choosing the next version to examine, not a claim that we have proved an overall usability improvement.
